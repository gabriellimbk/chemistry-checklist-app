# Effect of carbocation stability

1. State Markovnikov's rule and explain how it applies to the reaction of propene with hydrogen chloride.
Answer: Markovnikov's rule states that in the electrophilic addition of a protic acid H-X to an unsymmetrical alkene, the hydrogen atom adds to the carbon atom of the double bond that already has the greater number of hydrogen atoms, while the X group adds to the carbon atom with fewer hydrogen atoms. In the reaction of propene with hydrogen chloride, the hydrogen adds to the terminal carbon of the double bond (CH2) to form the more stable secondary carbocation, leading to 2-chloropropane as the major product.

2. Predict the major product when but-1-ene reacts with hydrogen bromide and explain your reasoning.
Answer: The major product will be 2-bromobutane. This is because the addition of HBr to but-1-ene will form either a primary carbocation (butan-1-yl carbocation) or a secondary carbocation (butan-2-yl carbocation). The secondary carbocation is more stable due to the inductive effect of two alkyl groups, and therefore it is formed more rapidly and in greater quantity, leading to 2-bromobutane as the major product.

3. Besides the inductive effect, what other factor can stabilise carbocations, particularly in conjugated systems?
Answer: Resonance stabilisation can also stabilise carbocations. If the positive charge can be delocalised over multiple atoms through pi bonds in a conjugated system, the carbocation becomes more stable. An example is the allyl carbocation.
