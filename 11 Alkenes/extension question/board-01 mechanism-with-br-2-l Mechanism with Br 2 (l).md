# Mechanism

1. Explain how the reaction of propene with HBr would differ from the reaction with Br two, focusing on the intermediate formed and the major product.
Answer: When propene reacts with HBr, the initial electrophilic attack by H plus forms a carbocation. According to Markovnikov's rule, the hydrogen adds to the carbon with more hydrogens already attached, forming the more stable secondary carbocation. The bromide ion then attacks this secondary carbocation to form the major product, two bromopropane. This demonstrates regioselectivity, where one isomer is preferentially formed.

2. What is the role of the solvent in electrophilic addition reactions of alkenes, particularly when using Br two?
Answer: Electrophilic addition reactions of alkenes with Br two are often carried out in non-polar solvents like tetrachloromethane, C Cl four. The solvent's role is to dissolve both the alkene and the bromine, allowing them to react. It does not participate in the reaction mechanism itself, as it is a non-polar solvent and does not stabilise ions as effectively as polar protic solvents would, which could interfere with the carbocation intermediate.

3. Why is the reaction of alkenes with Br two used as a test for unsaturation?
Answer: Alkenes react with bromine water or liquid bromine, causing the characteristic orange-brown colour of bromine to disappear as it is consumed in the addition reaction. Alkanes, being saturated, do not undergo this reaction under normal conditions. Therefore, the decolourisation of bromine serves as a visual test to confirm the presence of a carbon-carbon double bond, indicating unsaturation.
