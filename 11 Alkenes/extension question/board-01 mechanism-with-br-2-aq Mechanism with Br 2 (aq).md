# Mechanism

1. Explain why 1-bromo-2-methylpropan-2-ol is the major product, rather than 2-bromo-2-methylpropan-1-ol, in this reaction.
Answer: The initial electrophilic attack by bromine forms the most stable carbocation intermediate. In this case, the tertiary carbocation, where the positive charge is on the carbon bonded to two methyl groups and the bromine, is significantly more stable than a primary carbocation. The subsequent nucleophilic attack by water occurs at this more stable tertiary carbon, leading predominantly to 1-bromo-2-methylpropan-2-ol.

2. What would be the major product if the reaction was carried out with HBr instead of Br two in the presence of water, and briefly outline the mechanism?
Answer: If the reaction was carried out with HBr, the major product would be 2-bromo-2-methylpropane. The mechanism would involve the initial electrophilic addition of H plus to the alkene to form the most stable tertiary carbocation, followed by nucleophilic attack of the bromide ion on this carbocation.

3. How would the mechanism differ if the alkene was ethene instead of 2-methylpropene, reacting with Br two in the presence of water?
Answer: For ethene, the initial electrophilic addition of Br two would form a primary carbocation, which is less stable than a tertiary carbocation. However, due to the symmetrical nature of ethene, only one type of carbocation intermediate is possible. The subsequent nucleophilic attack by water and deprotonation would lead to 2-bromoethanol. The key difference is the symmetry of the alkene and the resulting stability of the intermediate carbocation.
