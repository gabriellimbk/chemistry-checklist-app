# Working backwards from strong oxidation

1. Contrast the products formed from an alkene undergoing strong oxidation with those formed from mild oxidation (using cold, dilute, alkaline potassium permanganate).
Answer: Mild oxidation, also known as Baeyer's test, adds two hydroxyl groups across the double bond, forming a diol, without cleaving the carbon-carbon double bond. Strong oxidation, however, completely cleaves the double bond, leading to carboxylic acids, ketones, or carbon dioxide depending on the substitution pattern of the alkene carbons.

2. How would the products of strong oxidation differ if the starting material was an alkyne instead of an alkene?
Answer: If the starting material was an alkyne, strong oxidation would cleave the carbon-carbon triple bond. A terminal alkyne, R-C triple bond CH, would yield a carboxylic acid, RCOOH, and carbon dioxide, CO2. An internal alkyne, R-C triple bond C-R', would yield two carboxylic acids, RCOOH and R'COOH.

3. Suggest another reagent system that could achieve similar oxidative cleavage of alkenes, producing carboxylic acids and ketones.
Answer: Ozonolysis followed by an oxidative workup, for example using hydrogen peroxide, can achieve similar oxidative cleavage of alkenes, producing carboxylic acids and ketones. A reductive workup, such as with zinc and water or dimethyl sulfide, would produce aldehydes and ketones instead.
