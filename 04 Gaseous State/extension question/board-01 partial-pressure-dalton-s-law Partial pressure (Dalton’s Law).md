# Partial pressure (Dalton’s Law)

1. What is the relationship between the partial pressure of a gas and its mole fraction in a mixture?
Answer: The partial pressure of a gas is directly proportional to its mole fraction in the mixture. Specifically, the partial pressure of a gas equals its mole fraction multiplied by the total pressure of the gas mixture.

2. Explain why Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures is applicable to ideal gases but may show deviations for real gases, especially at high pressures.
Answer: Dalton's Law assumes that gas molecules do not interact with each other and occupy negligible volume, which are characteristics of ideal gases. For real gases, especially at high pressures, intermolecular forces become significant, and the volume occupied by the gas molecules themselves is no longer negligible. These interactions and molecular volumes affect the collision frequency and force with the container walls, causing deviations from ideal behavior and thus from Dalton's Law.

3. If the temperature of the vessel containing nitrogen and oxygen were increased, how would the partial pressure of nitrogen change, assuming the volume remains constant?
Answer: If the temperature of the vessel were increased, the partial pressure of nitrogen would also increase. According to the Ideal Gas Law (P V = n R T), for a fixed number of moles of nitrogen and constant volume, the partial pressure of nitrogen is directly proportional to the absolute temperature. Higher temperature means gas molecules have higher kinetic energy, leading to more frequent and forceful collisions with the container walls, thus increasing the partial pressure.
