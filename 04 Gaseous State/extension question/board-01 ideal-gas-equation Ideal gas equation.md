# Ideal gas equation

1. Explain why real gases deviate from ideal behaviour, particularly at high pressures and low temperatures.
Answer: Real gases deviate from ideal behaviour because the ideal gas equation assumes that gas particles have negligible volume and no intermolecular forces. At high pressures, the volume of the gas particles themselves becomes significant compared to the total volume, and at low temperatures, the kinetic energy of the particles is reduced, making intermolecular forces more significant, causing particles to attract each other and reducing the effective pressure.

2. Calculate the volume occupied by 2.0 moles of an ideal gas at 25 degrees Celsius and a pressure of 100 kPa. (R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1).
Answer: First, convert temperature to Kelvin: T = 25 + 273 = 298 K. Convert pressure to Pascals: P = 100 kPa = 100 000 Pa. Using pV = nRT, rearrange to V = nRT/p. V = (2.0 mol * 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1 * 298 K) / 100 000 Pa. V = 0.0495 m^3 (or 49.5 dm^3).

3. State two assumptions made when deriving the ideal gas equation from the kinetic theory of gases.
Answer: The two main assumptions are: 1) The volume of the gas particles themselves is negligible compared to the total volume occupied by the gas. 2) There are no attractive or repulsive forces between the gas particles (i.e., intermolecular forces are negligible).
