# Graph sketching

1. Why do real gases deviate from ideal gas behaviour, especially at high pressures and low temperatures?
Answer: Real gases deviate because the ideal gas law assumes gas particles have negligible volume and no intermolecular forces. At high pressures, particles are closer, so their volume becomes significant, and at low temperatures, kinetic energy is reduced, making intermolecular forces more significant, causing particles to attract and reduce pressure.

2. How can the ideal gas equation be rearranged to determine the molar mass of an unknown gas?
Answer: The ideal gas equation is pV = nRT. We know n = mass / molar mass (m/M). Substituting this gives pV = (m/M)RT. Rearranging for M gives M = (mRT) / (pV). By measuring the mass, pressure, volume, and temperature of a gas, its molar mass can be calculated.

3. If a mixture of two ideal gases, A and B, is in a container, how does the total pressure relate to the partial pressures of A and B?
Answer: According to Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures, the total pressure exerted by a mixture of non-reacting ideal gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of the individual gases. So, P total = P A + P B, where P A and P B are the partial pressures of gas A and gas B, respectively.
