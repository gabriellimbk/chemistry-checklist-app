# Deviation from ideal gas

1. How would the graph for helium, He, compare to that of F two and Cl two at the same temperature?
Answer: Helium is a much smaller atom with a very small electron cloud, resulting in extremely weak London dispersion forces and a negligible molecular volume. Therefore, its graph would show very little deviation from ideal gas behavior, staying much closer to the ideal gas line (pV/RT = 1) across all pressures compared to both F two and Cl two. It would exhibit the least negative deviation at moderate pressures and the least positive deviation at very high pressures.

2. Explain why the ideal gas law, pV = nRT, is a good approximation for the behavior of real gases at high temperatures and low pressures.
Answer: At high temperatures, gas molecules have high kinetic energy, meaning they move very fast and the attractive forces between them are less significant compared to their kinetic energy. At low pressures, the molecules are far apart, so their actual volume is negligible compared to the total volume of the container, and intermolecular forces are minimal. Under these conditions, the two main assumptions of the ideal gas law (negligible molecular volume and no intermolecular forces) hold true, making it a good approximation.

3. What are the two main assumptions of the kinetic theory of gases that are violated by real gases, leading to deviations from ideal behavior?
Answer: The two main assumptions violated are: 1. The volume occupied by the gas molecules themselves is negligible compared to the total volume of the container. Real gas molecules do occupy a finite volume. 2. There are no attractive or repulsive forces between gas molecules. Real gas molecules do experience intermolecular forces of attraction and repulsion.
