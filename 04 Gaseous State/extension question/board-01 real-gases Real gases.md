# Real gases

1. What are the two main assumptions made about particles in an ideal gas?
Answer: The two main assumptions are that the volume occupied by the gas particles themselves is negligible compared to the total volume of the container, and that there are no intermolecular forces of attraction or repulsion between the gas particles.

2. How does the van der Waals equation modify the ideal gas law to account for the finite volume of gas particles and the presence of intermolecular forces?
Answer: The van der Waals equation modifies the ideal gas law, PV = nRT, by introducing two correction terms. To account for the finite volume of particles, a term 'nb' is subtracted from the volume V, where 'b' represents the excluded volume per mole. To account for intermolecular forces, a term 'a(n/V)squared' is added to the pressure P, where 'a' relates to the strength of the attractive forces. The modified equation is (P + a(n/V)squared)(V - nb) = nRT.

3. Which types of real gas molecules would you expect to deviate most significantly from ideal gas behaviour, and why?
Answer: Real gas molecules that are large in size and have strong intermolecular forces, such as polar molecules or those with significant London dispersion forces, would deviate most significantly. Larger molecules occupy a greater finite volume, and stronger intermolecular forces lead to more pronounced attractions between particles, both of which contradict the assumptions of an ideal gas. For example, gases like ammonia or water vapour would deviate more than helium or hydrogen.
