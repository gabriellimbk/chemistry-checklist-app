# Ideal gas assumptions

1. Under what conditions of temperature and pressure do real gases behave most like ideal gases?
Answer: Real gases behave most like ideal gases at high temperatures and low pressures. At high temperatures, particles have high kinetic energy, overcoming intermolecular forces. At low pressures, particles are far apart, so their volume is negligible compared to the container, and intermolecular forces are minimal.

2. Explain why the assumption of negligible particle volume breaks down at high pressures.
Answer: At high pressures, the gas particles are forced closer together, significantly reducing the total volume of the container. In this scenario, the actual volume occupied by the gas particles themselves becomes a significant fraction of the total container volume and can no longer be considered negligible.

3. How does the presence of strong intermolecular forces in a real gas affect its pressure compared to an ideal gas under the same conditions?
Answer: If a real gas has strong intermolecular forces, these attractive forces pull the particles towards each other, reducing the frequency and force of collisions with the container walls. Consequently, the measured pressure of the real gas will be lower than that predicted for an ideal gas under the same conditions.
